# Abstract
- Synthesizing 30 peer-reviewed studies on RAG.
- Focusing on diagnostic support, electronic health record (EHR)
summarization, and medical question answering.
- Synthesize the existing architectural variants (naïve, advanced, and
modular) and examine their deployment across these applications.
- Challenges:
+ Retrieval noise
+ Domain shift (performance degradation when models are applied to data
distributions different from their training set)
+ Generation latency
+ Limited explainability
- Using both standard metrics and clinical-specific metrics for 
evaluation.

# 2. Methodology
- A comprehensive search was performed in PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, 
Web of Science, Google Scholar, and ACM Digital Library, covering 
publications from 2022 to 2025.

## 2.2. Data Retrieval and Screening
- This review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items
for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines.
- 30 studies were included.

# 4. Related Works
## 4.2. RAG Chatbots for Patient Interaction
- Kulshreshtha et al. propose a RAG-based medical chatbot using 
standalone LLMs (LLaMa, LangChain, and BERT). It uses context-aware 
embeddings, Faiss for retrieval. The methodology includes tokenization
, retrieval, beam search, and post-processing. There are gaps in 
multilingual support.
- Sree et al. introduce a RAG-based LLM chatbot that employs agents 
and modular tools for domain-specific retrieval and semantic 
understanding, using indexing and embedding-based search to generate 
real-time, relevant outputs. However, it lacks clinical validation, 
deployment benchmarks, error analysis.

## 4.5. Graph-Based and Ontology-Aware RAG Frameworks
- Wu et al. introduce a graph-based RAG method for medical question
answering. It improves reliability by linking user data to trusted
medical sources using Triple Graph Construction and retrieves answers
through a two-step U-Retrieval process. Tested on benchmarks, it 
outperforms standard RAG, GraphRAG [56], and fine-tuned medical LLMs.
However, challenges remain in adapting to live data, complex patient 
queries, and maintaining explainability.

# 5. Applications
## 5.3. Medical Question Answering
- Retrieving content from curated biomedical corpora (e.g., PubMedQA).
- Anchoring answers in peer-reviewed literature or structured medical 
knowledge, these systems reduce the hallucinations often
seen in standalone LLMs. 
- Indexing strategies like BM25 ensure relevance in long-form
or open-ended queries, improving both reliability and transparency.

# 6. Evaluation Framework: Metrics and Benchmarks
## 6.1. Domain-Specific Metrics for Clinical Validation
- FactScore
- RadGraph-F1
- MED-F1

## 6.2. Generation Quality Metrics
- Natural language generation (NLG) metrics are employed to assess
the textual quality of model outputs:
+ BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy)
+ ROUGE (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation)
+ F1 Score
+ BERTScore

## 6.3. Retrieval Relevance Metrics
- Evaluating the relevance and ranking of retrieved documents:
+ Recall@k
+ Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)
+ Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG@k)

# 7. Challenges and Limitations
- Include retrieval accuracy, generation latency, limited
explainability, and data privacy concerns.
