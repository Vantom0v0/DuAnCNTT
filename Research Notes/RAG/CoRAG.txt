# Notes
## Abstract
- CoRAG teaches a language model to think, retrieve, and reason 
step-by-step—mirroring an "o1-like" multi-step problem-solving 
process before producing a final answer.

## Method
- Data Augmentation via Rejection Sampling: Because standard RAG 
datasets only contain a query Q and a final answer A (lacking 
intermediate steps), CoRAG automatically generates step-by-step 
retrieval chains for training using rejection sampling. It generates 
chains of sub-queries (Q_1…Q_L) and sub-answers (A_1…A_L). The chain 
that achieves the highest conditional log-likelihood of producing the 
correct final answer is selected to augment the dataset.
- Multi-Task Training: An open-source LLM is fine-tuned (
full-parameter) on these augmented datasets using standard next-token 
prediction. The model is simultaneously trained on three tasks: next 
sub-query prediction, sub-answer prediction, and final answer 
prediction.
- Inference-Time Scaling: At test time, CoRAG can scale its compute 
budget by controlling the retrieval chain length (L) and deploying 
different decoding strategies:
+ Greedy Decoding: Generates sub-queries and sub-answers sequentially.
+ Best-of-N Sampling: Samples N chains and ranks them using a penalty 
score (calculating the conditional log-likelihood of generating "No 
relevant information found" when retrieving), selecting the chain 
with the lowest penalty.
+ Tree Search (BFS): Explores a tree of sub-queries, performing 
multiple rollouts and retaining the states with the lowest average 
penalty scores.
- Learning to Stop: During the chain, the model is trained to predict a 
"Yes" or "No" early-stopping token to judge whether the accumulated 
information is sufficient to generate the final response.
