# 2. RAG LLMs
## 2.1. Retrieval
- Dense and sparse retrieval
- Sparse is straight forward, find using the words (TF-IDF or BM25).
It can not be trained.
- Dense uses embedding. Trainable: 
+ A common design is to use bi-encoder (one for query and one for 
document). 
+ One typical success is Dense Passage Retriever (DPR).
+ Bi-encoders are also applied in ICL-based RAG (In context learning).
+ One-encoder retrievers (e.g. Contriver and Spider) are effective in
many RAG LLM methods, such as In-context RALM, Atlas, and Self-RAG.
- Retrieval granularity;
+ Early RAG retrives the whole document.
+ Chunk retrieval is the common method now.
+ There is also token revieval which is faster but it is costly for the 
database.
+ Another major retrieval method is entity retrieval.
+ Another study propose to build the knowledge base by learning and 
retrieving mention rather than entity

## 2.2 Generation
- Using black-box generation models are the mainstream method for
generator. (i.e. Using close-soure models from GPT, Claude, etc.)
- Black-box RA-LLMs, therefore, focus more on the retrieval and 
augmentation processes, trying to enhance the generator by prompting.

## 2.3 Retrieval Integration for Generation Augmentation
- Input-layer Intergration: combine documents with query before 
passing into an LLM or generator. In general, most black-box 
generation-based RAG methods apply input-layer integration
- Output-layer: combine the model's answer with the retrieved 
information.
- Intermediate: inject information inside the model.

# 3. Training
## 3.1. Training-free
- Insert retrieved text into the prompt
- 2 categories: 
+ Prompt-engineering RAG: retrieved/generated text goes into the prompt
, no weights touched. Variants: standard concat (In-Context RALM), 
interleave with reasoning steps instead of one-shot retrieval (IRCoT),
skip retrieval and have the LLM generate its own context (GENREAD), let
the model decide whether to even bother retrieving (SKR), decompose 
ambiguous questions before retrieving by constructing a tree (TOC).
+ Retrieval-guided token generation: retrieval intervenes at 
output/token level rather than input. kNN-LM — blends LM's next-token 
distribution with a distribution built from similar past contexts 
(accuracy/grounding). Rest — swaps the draft model in speculative 
decoding for a retrieval datastore (speed, not accuracy).

## 3.2. Independent Training
- Train the LLMs and retriever seperately

## 3.3. Sequential Training
- To better enhance the synergy between LLms and retrievers.
- Independently pretrain either the retriever or the LLMs --> keep
the pretrained module fixed while training the other module.
- 2 categories: retriever first or LLMs first
- Retriever first: train retriever --> freeze --> train LLMs with the
retrieved knowledge.
- LLMs first: train LLMs --> tune the retriever under the supervision 
of the LLMs.

## 3.4. Joint Training
- Train both simultaneously.
